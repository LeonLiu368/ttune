# 03 – 2026 trends in RL environments

Collected September 2026 from search snippets of X posts and the blogs and papers they link to. I couldn't open X directly, so treat the X items as summaries of posts, not full reads.

## What people are saying

1. **Coding is saturated, other domains are wide open.** Coding still gets the most lab spending because its rewards are easy to verify, and terminal tasks are now generated automatically at scale (Endless Terminals, SETA, FACET). Domains called underserved: manufacturing, biology, logistics, medicine, enterprise workflows. Chip design, tax accounting and electronic health records are named specifically.
2. **Depth beats breadth.** Most environments are called brittle, single-app and partly hand-graded. Mechanize's pitch is a few robust environments rather than many simple ones.
3. **Reward hacking is the big quality concern.** Anthropic reported finding 80 environments vulnerable to reward hacking. SWE-Marathon saw ~14% of rollouts try to exploit the verifier. There are new benchmarks just for hack-resistance (SpecBench, Hack-Verifiable Environments).
4. **Long-horizon tasks are the new frontier.** SWE-Marathon and LongHorizon-Harness test multi-hour, many-step work.
5. **Procedurally generated environments.** Nathan Lambert: 2026 RL research is moving from benchmark-gaming toward procedural environment generation and whether RL generalizes across domains.
6. **Money and crowding.** Anthropic has reportedly discussed spending more than $1B a year on environments; there are 40+ environment startups. The market is expected to consolidate around a few trusted partners.
7. **Harbor's registry is large but mostly terminal/software:** about 80 datasets and 51k tasks.

## What this means for my ideas

- Tax prep, accounting close and clinical records (see [02](02-realistic-environments.md)) match exactly what's called underserved.
- PLC programming and PCB fixes fit the manufacturing and chip-design gap, and simulator-based checkers are hard to game.
- **Build a task generator, not a handful of hand-made tasks** (trends 2 and 5).
- **Make hack-resistance a selling point:** keep tests and ground truth out of the agent's reach, check outcomes rather than output format, and document red-team attempts in each task README.
